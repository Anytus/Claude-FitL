Teacher pilot: results (plan in pilot_plan.txt; labels pilot_labels.jsonl; tools/teacher.py output pilot_summary.txt)

52 decisions (32 disagree, 20 other), 31,200 playouts to the end of the game in 40 minutes
on 4 cores (0.24 s a playout on one core; step 2's 0.52 s was measured with all four
processes labelling at once in short games, so it overstated the cost); 0.8% of playouts
failed (program assertion or Bot loop). Identical-games check of the reseed build: 0 of
236 games differ.

Noise: correlation of the paired playouts' wins 0.19 (0.12 without the per-step reseed);
sd of a paired win difference 0.55, so one decision's difference at 200 playouts has se
about 4 points, and about 750 playouts per candidate are needed for se 2.

H1 (disagree decisions, Event - N1, positive = Event weight 0 right): mean +1.6 points of
win chance (se 3.2); median +0.2; 16 better, 15 worse. One decision carries it: Americal
(#21) in the final campaign, 98.5% against 6.0% (+92.5); without it the mean is -1.3
(se 1.4). Not supported as stated: the typical disagreement is a close call; the mean is
positive only through one decisive Event. The paired game test's +3.7 points a game would
be matched if about one disagreement in 30 were worth +90 (1.5 disagreements a game), which
is what the pilot happened to find, but one case cannot establish a rate.

H2 (N1 - N2, the policy's own ranking): +1.9 (se 1.5) on disagree decisions, +4.2 (se 2.6)
on the others. Weakly supported: the policy's first choice beats its second on average.
Other decisions, Event - N1: -7.3 (se 2.6): where the policy ranks the Event well below
its first choice, the Event is worse.

H3: correlation of policy score differences with playout win differences over 156
candidate pairs: 0.22; same sign in 87 of 147 (59%).

Not pre-registered:
- Held-out regret of the policy's pick against the playout-best of its 3 candidates
  (best chosen on one half of the playouts, measured on the other): mean +1.9 points per
  decision, median 0.0.
- Decisions whose best and worst candidates differ by more than 20 points: 7 of 52
  (more than 10: 18). The large ones: Americal +92.5 (Event right), Pathet Lao -32.5,
  #4 -34.0, #11 -25.0 and -24.0, #46 -20.5 (Event wrong).

Conclusions:
1. The playouts can detect differences of 10-20 points or more at single decisions at 200
   playouts each; they cannot rank close calls (se 4) without hundreds more.
2. Most decisions in this sample are close calls; the value lies in a few decisive ones
   (here about 1 in 7 differ by more than 20 points).
3. The policy's score is informative but weak (correlation 0.22 with the playouts).
4. The pilot neither confirms nor refutes the Event result on its own: the sign is right
   only through one decision.
