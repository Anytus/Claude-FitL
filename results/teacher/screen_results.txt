Conclusions:
1. Within the menu the search ranks (the best of each kind), the policy's choice is as
   good as the playout-best alternative on average (+0.2, se 0.3). Re-ranking that menu,
   by weights or by playouts at decision time, has almost nothing to gain, which fits the
   flat response of win rate to weights.
2. The policy's score is a weak predictor of which candidate is better (0.16), yet its
   picks lose almost nothing: the candidates it chooses between are mostly worth about the
   same.
3. So the gap to the LLM is outside this menu: how each Operation is carried out (spaces,
   pieces: the Tru'ng routines decide), the Coup-round decisions (Tru'ng routines), and
   candidates the menu does not generate. Or in the rare decisive positions, such as a
   Failed Attempt on deck.
Caveats: the alternatives are only the best of each kind by the policy's own score;
the playouts use the same policy, so this measures one step of improvement only.
