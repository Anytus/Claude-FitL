Reanalysis of the menu and space-choice screenings: how far apart the alternatives truly are
(tools/teacher_spread.py; output in spread_summary.txt)

Why: the screenings' main measure, held-out regret (best candidate picked on 50 playouts,
measured on the other 50), is what a chooser with 50 noisy playouts would gain. With noise
of about 7 points per candidate on 50 playouts, that chooser mostly picks noise, so the
measure says little about how much better the best alternative truly is. The plans noted
that it pulls towards 0; the results files then read it as "nothing to gain". That reading
was wrong for the menu and for the Coup Pacify.

Method: for each alternative, the paired win difference against the bot's choice is a noisy
estimate of the true difference. The true spread across alternatives is estimated two ways,
which agree: observed variance minus sampling variance, and the covariance between the
estimates from the two halves of the playouts (independent noise, same true difference);
the latter also without the top and bottom 1% of alternatives, to see how much of the spread
is a few extreme decisions. A perfect chooser's gain among the tested alternatives is then
computed under a normal model.

                         alternatives  mean alt - choice  true sd (trimmed)  perfect choice gains
Menu (other kinds)            982           -2.2              6.4 (4.4)        +3.7 (+2.1) a decision
Space, US turns (1 pick)      822           -0.8              2.7 (0.3)        +1.6 (+0.0)
Space, Coup Pacify (1 pick)   182           +0.5              3.6 (3.1)        +3.0 (+2.6)
(points of win chance; half-to-half correlation of the estimates: menu 0.41, turns 0.13,
Coup 0.19)

Corrected conclusions:
1. Which kind of action to take matters: alternatives differ from the bot's choice by
   4-6 points of win chance (sd) in a typical decision. The bot's choice is better than the
   average alternative (by 2.2) but in many decisions not the best; choosing perfectly among
   just the 2-3 alternatives tested would gain about 2-4 points a decision.
2. Coup Support-phase Pacify: where the US Pacifies matters by about 3 points (sd) per
   alternative, broadly, not only in rare cases; the Tru'ng ordering is no better than a
   random eligible space (+0.5); perfect choice among 2-3 random alternatives would gain
   about 2.5-3 points a Coup.
3. One space pick inside a US action: for typical decisions the changes are truly
   near-equal (sd 0.3 without the extreme 1%); the spread is in a few decisive positions
   (such as the Train before a Coup in seed 922011).
4. So decisions matter; what the screenings showed is that the bot cannot tell which
   alternative is better: its score correlates about 0.16 with the playout differences,
   and 100 playouts per candidate leave noise of about 5 points per comparison.
These gains are among the alternatives tested and judged by the bot's own continuation;
they are upper bounds for what an evaluator could realize there, and they do not add up
simply across a game's decisions.
